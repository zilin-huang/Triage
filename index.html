01 / THE PROBLEM

The top-ranked checkpoint
may not run on your platform.

Teams that cannot train a foundation model pick a public checkpoint by benchmark rank and fine-tune it on local data. The platform is fixed first: its sensors, onboard compute and site come with the task.

We split what a checkpoint loses on a fixed platform by what can repair it. A sensing gap is information the sensors do not capture; no amount of their data closes it. A latency gap is output that arrives after the control loop needs it; it belongs to the loop, not the weights. Only a distribution gap is fine-tuning’s to repair.

TRIAGE turns this split into a five-stage procedure: two gates that no score overrides, a ranking among the candidates that pass, and fine-tuning that is monitored and checked in closed loop before release.

02 / THE APPROACH

Gate first. Rank second.
Fine-tune under watch.

01

Gate on sensing and latency

Stage 1 checks the model’s sensor contract against the platform’s cameras. Stage 2 checks the end-to-end latency at the 95th percentile, measured on the deployment hardware, against the control deadline. A failing gate names the repair it calls for.

02

Probe, then rank

Stage 3 runs probes on ten minutes of local logs. Stage 4 ranks the candidates that pass both gates by target-relevant benchmark score and keeps the runner-up as challenger.

03

Adapt under monitoring

Stage 5 fine-tunes on local data while monitoring the robustness it can cost, and releases a model only after a closed-loop check.

STAGE 1 / SENSOR CONTRACT

Field of view is gated.
Resolution is not.

At equal pixels, losing field of view moves plans 2.2 to 5.7 times more than losing resolution (1.6 to 2.4 in manipulation), and all four Bench2Drive models score lower under the crop than under the downscale in closed loop.

On the vehicle, the factory 24.7° camera failed every sighted sensor contract; one added 102° camera, reprojected to a 64° view, re-admitted the six monocular candidates.

STAGE 5 / MONITORED ADAPTATION

IN THE AUTHORS’ WORDS

Abstract

Embodied foundation models are increasingly deployed by selection: a practitioner picks a public checkpoint by benchmark rank and fine-tunes it on local data. The platform is fixed before the model is chosen: its sensors, onboard compute and operating site are set by the task, and at the start there is little or no local data. We ask what such a checkpoint loses on a fixed physical platform and which part of the loss local data can buy back. Our answer is to split the gap by what can repair it. A sensing gap, information the sensors do not capture, cannot be closed with their data; a latency gap, output arriving after the control loop needs it, belongs to the loop, not the weights; only a distribution gap is fine-tuning’s to repair. We turn this into TRIAGE, a procedure for feasibility-gated selection and monitored adaptation in five stages: gates on the sensor contract and on end-to-end latency that no score can override, probes on ten minutes of local logs, a ranking by target-relevant score among the candidates that pass, and fine-tuning that is monitored and checked in closed loop before release. On driving benchmarks, a deadline below a leading closed-loop model’s latency makes it collide on 80 to 90% of routes, and losing field of view moves plans 2.2 to 5.7 times more than losing resolution at equal pixels (1.6 to 2.4 in manipulation) and costs more driving score in closed loop. Local frames close 75% of a stability gap but raise perturbation sensitivity at small budgets, fine-tuning removes more of a moderate crop’s cost than of a strong crop’s, and picking by score violates a gate in two of four deployment profiles. On a full-scale vehicle, the factory camera failed the sensing gate and an added camera repaired it. The latency gate, applied to the end-to-end latency measured on board, removed the two highest-scoring candidates, neither of which completed a closed-loop trip. The selected model passed closed-loop acceptance in 4 of 5 trips, and the closed-loop check withheld a fine-tuned model that had improved offline. TRIAGE lets organizations that cannot train foundation models from scratch find out, before buying data or hardware, whether a public checkpoint can run on the robot or vehicle they own, and what to fix if not.
